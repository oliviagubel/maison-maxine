The Wine Spritz Guide

What a wine spritz is, where the red wine spritz fits in, and how to pour ours.

What is a wine spritz?

A wine spritz is wine lengthened with something sparkling, so it's lighter, fizzier and lower in alcohol than a glass of wine on its own. The name comes from the German spritzen, "to splash".

Spritzer, Aperol spritz, red wine spritz: what's the difference?

  • A wine spritzer is wine topped up with soda water, usually white wine.
  • An Aperol spritz is an Italian aperitif: Aperol, Prosecco and a splash of soda.
  • A red wine spritz starts with red wine instead. Maison Maxine is a red wine spritz made with Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon, ginger, lemon and honey, inspired by red wine and ginger ale.

What's in Maison Maxine?

Four ingredients: Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon, natural ginger flavoring, lemon and honey, with no refined sugar. It's lightly sparkling, 5% ABV, comes in 250 mL (8.4 fl oz) cans, and is certified organic by CCOF.

How strong is it compared with wine?

Maison Maxine is 5% alcohol by volume. Red wine is typically around 12 to 15%. A 250 mL can at 5% holds about 12.5 mL (roughly 10 g) of alcohol, about 0.7 of a U.S. standard drink, which is 14 g. Please enjoy responsibly; 21+ only.

How to serve it

  • Chill it, or pour it over ice.
  • Drink it from the can, or pour it into a wine glass to see the color.
  • Good for golden hour, girls' night, picnics, date night and any Tuesday.
